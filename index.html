            How ScreenGuard Works, and What We've Tested

            What each check does, and the evidence behind it

            Most employers run applications through software such as Workday, Greenhouse or Lever. It reads your CV into a profile, compares it with the job, and sometimes ranks it with AI. A recruiter then searches and skims what's left. ScreenGuard looks for the problems that commonly trip CVs up along the way.

            ScreenGuard can't see how any particular employer has set things up, so use its scores as a rough guide. Each section below says what we have tested and what we haven't.

            1 Can the software read your CV?

            If the software can't read your CV properly, no person ever sees it. ScreenGuard checks the file itself (.pdf or .docx) as well as the words in it.

            Scanned or picture-only PDFs

            Some design tools save the whole CV as a picture. The software then sees a blank page.

            Columns, tables and images

            Two-column layouts, Word tables and images are often skipped or read in the wrong order.

            Missing line breaks

            Sometimes headings, jobs and dates run together in one block. One click splits them back into lines. No words change.

            Contact details, symbols and length

            Checks that your email and phone number are in the main text, warns about decorative symbols, and flags CVs longer than 3 pages.

            What we've tested: these are well-known problems, but we haven't measured how often each one costs people an interview.

            2 How well do you match this job? (Job Fit)

            • Must-haves: ScreenGuard picks out the job's hard requirements, such as years of experience, a degree, specific tools, the right to work and location. Next to each, it shows the line of your CV that proves it. Many employers reject automatically when one of these is missing, so if a required item has no proof, the verdict can't go above Partial match.
            • The job's own words: recruiters usually search for the exact words in the job ad. If your CV says "customer feedback" and the job says "Voice of Customer", a search may miss you. ScreenGuard points these out.
            • Seniority and dates: compares the level of your latest job title with the role. It also looks for gaps of more than 6 months, jobs that overlap, dates without months, and dates written in different styles.

            What we've tested: we ran Job Fit on 1,759 CVs that had been labelled as a good, possible or poor match for a job. Take two CVs for the same job, one labelled good and one poor: Job Fit scored the good one higher about two times in three. A coin toss would manage one in two. It did not work well for comparing scores across different jobs. So by default the verdict says Strong, Partial or Weak match, and it doesn't tell you whether to expect an interview. Nobody knows who labelled that data or how, so even these results are rough.

            3 Does your CV read well? (CV Readiness)

            This second score looks at the CV on its own, whatever the job. It covers layout, results backed by numbers, and clear writing: action words instead of "responsible for", no filler, a sensible length. It's kept separate from Job Fit. In testing it told us almost nothing about whether a CV matched a job, and combining the two made the match harder to see. A very low Readiness score still lowers the verdict by one step, because a CV the software can't read properly may never be judged on its fit.

            4 Filling gaps with real experience

            When the job asks for something your CV doesn't show, ScreenGuard asks you about it. Have you done it, where, and what exactly did you do? It also asks for numbers behind bullets that have none. Only what you tell it goes into your CV, under the job you choose. Adding true information is the only honest way to close a gap, and we think it's the most useful thing the app does.

            What we've tested: nothing yet. Testing this needs real people and real applications, which is what the Tracker is for.

            5 Tailoring, and the truth check

            Tailoring reorders your CV and rewords it in the job's language. It works only from your master CV and your answers to the gap questions. After AI tailoring, ScreenGuard checks every line against those two sources. If the AI added a figure, skill, tool or qualification that isn't there, ScreenGuard takes it out or puts your original sentence back. The Truth Check lists everything it changed. If something it removed is true, you can put it back with one click. Before you export, ScreenGuard also warns you about leftover placeholders and anything you haven't confirmed.

            What we've tested: we took 100 CVs that were close to matching their job. One AI model tailored them and a different one judged them, without knowing which version was which. Honest AI tailoring raised the judge's match score by about 1.5 points out of 100. It made no real difference to how many CVs were put through to a phone screen: 8 before tailoring, 9 after. The app's offline tailoring made no difference at all.

            An earlier version, before these checks were added, scored higher. But about one CV in ten from that version included a skill, qualification or figure the person didn't have. That's why ScreenGuard now checks every line.

            In short: tailoring tidies up your CV but won't get you through on its own. Apply for jobs you match, make sure you meet the must-haves, and add real experience where your CV is thin.

            6 Learning from your own results

            The Tracker saves each application with the exact CV you sent and its scores, and you note what happened. Once you have 8 results, including at least 3 interviews and 3 rejections or no replies, it works out which Job Fit scores led to interviews for you. You can then use those cut-offs instead of the defaults. Your own results will tell you more than any test we can run.

            7 How the scores are worked out

            Your browser works out both scores, so checking the same CV twice gives the same result. If you connect an AI provider, Job Fit also takes into account the AI's view of how well your CV matches the job's keywords.

            Job Fit = 50% the job's exact terms + 30% words in common + 20% must-haves  |  CV Readiness = 38% layout + 34% results + 28% clarity

            What ScreenGuard can't see or promise

            ScreenGuard can't see an employer's screening questions, such as whether they sponsor visas or which locations and salaries they'll accept. It can't tell whether a job is already meant for someone, or how many people have applied. Some things no CV can fix: in one well-known study, identical CVs got different numbers of callbacks depending only on the name at the top. ScreenGuard can help you avoid rejections you can prevent and focus on jobs where you stand a chance. It can't guarantee you an interview.

            Your privacy and API keys

            Your CV, job ads, answers and Tracker stay in this browser. They're only sent to an AI provider if you choose to use one. Your API keys are saved in this browser too. They are encrypted, but the key needed to read them is saved in the same place, so this only stops someone casually looking. On a shared computer, choose "Session only" in AI settings and click Clear Data when you've finished.
